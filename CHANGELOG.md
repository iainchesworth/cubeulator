## [0.1.0-beta.1] - 2026-10-15

The first release of Cubeulator, a Rubik's Cube solver that reads the cube through a camera. Hold each side of a scrambled cube up to the camera and it works out the cube, solves it, and shows you the solution turn by turn on a 3D cube. For Windows, macOS and Linux, and in a browser.

### Added

- **Scan a cube by holding each side to the camera.** Show it the six sides, square on, and each fills its own chip on the scan screen in the colours the camera measured; when the sixth is read the cube is known and the solve begins. There is no cube to build by hand and nothing to type. See [docs/vision-pipeline.md](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Red and orange are told apart under warm light.** Under a tungsten bulb the two are only a few degrees of hue apart, so a name is not enough. The scan lets the cube decide: only one naming of the colours makes a cube that can exist, and that is the one it keeps.
- **Three ways to solve it, picked on the solve screen.** *Beginner* works layer by layer and takes about 100 moves; *Intermediate* is the CFOP method, about 50; *Advanced* is Kociemba's two-phase search, about 20. Beginner is the default. Each answers in about a second or less on a desktop. See [docs/solver-methods.md](docs/solver-methods.md).
- **The solution is shown on a 3D cube, not spelt out.** The layer the next move turns nudges a little way in the direction it has to go, and back (twice for a half turn); stepping turns it all the way. A move on the back swings the view round behind the cube first, and says that it is the view that has turned. You do not need to read `R U R' U'`.
- **The camera follows the solve.** It stays on after the scan. A side held square to it is matched against the cube as it should stand, and the 3D cube turns to match your hold. When the side in view fits the cube after the move asked for, the solution steps on by itself; when it fits a different turn, the screen names the side you turned. The buttons and the keys step whenever the camera cannot see the move.
- **Settings.** Which camera to use, which method solves, the appearance (follow the system, light, or dark), and the language; also how the 3D cube is shaded and whether it turns by itself, and which graphics system draws the window. The method, the appearance, the language and the graphics system are remembered. See [docs/guide/settings.md](docs/guide/settings.md).
- **Nine languages, written right to left where they should be.** English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish can be chosen in Settings and apply at once: layout direction, numbers and plurals included. Source text is British English. German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish are translated, as first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through yet: expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. Japanese and Chinese are each set in their own system face (Yu Gothic UI and Microsoft YaHei UI on Windows, Hiragino Sans and PingFang SC on macOS, Noto Sans CJK on Linux, which the packages recommend; the browser build carries its own). German, French and Spanish show English text until their translations are written, and in every language the scan screen's guidance and most of the words round the solution are still English.
- **Installers for the three desktops.** A Windows installer (Windows 10 and later, 64-bit), a macOS disk image (macOS 14.4 and later, Apple Silicon), and Debian and Fedora packages for Linux. Each carries the app icon, its version, and the licence and the notices for everything it ships. See [docs/packaging.md](docs/packaging.md).
- **It runs in a browser, too.** [Try it in your browser](docs/web.md) opens the same app as WebAssembly in a tab, with nothing to install: the camera, the scan, the three solve methods and the 3D cube. Nothing it sees leaves the device. One difference from the desktop apps: the camera's exposure and white balance are not locked for a scan, so scan under steady light. It carries its own Japanese and Simplified Chinese faces (a browser has no system fonts), cut down to the characters the app uses. It is published with the documentation, under `/app/`.
- **Windows, macOS and Linux behave like apps there.** The window opens at 960 by 680, remembers its size, position and whether it was maximised (and ignores a position that is off every screen), has keyboard shortcuts for Settings, a new scan, full screen and quit, and gets the macOS menu bar.
- **Every part the app is made from is credited.** The third-party notices are generated by the build from what ships, installed with every package and embedded in the app; a test fails if something that ships has no notice. The licence inventory is in [docs/licensing.md](docs/licensing.md).
- **A profiler build for developers.** The same Tracy instrumentation as the rest of the application family: zones for the camera thread, the scan, the solver stages and the Qt Quick frame, off by default and compiled to nothing. See [docs/profiling.md](docs/profiling.md).
- **Performance tests for developers.** A change that makes the solver or the scan meaningfully slower now fails a test, the way a wrong one does. The checks count what the code does - search nodes visited, pixel buffers allocated - so they are exact on any machine and run with the ordinary tests; the wall-time checks are opt-in and loose by design. See [docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#performance-tests).
- **The scan says which corner to show when a cube is ambiguous.** When the six sides you showed fit together in more than one way - patterned cubes do - the screen no longer only asks for "two sides at once": it names the corner whose view would settle it, by the colours of the three sides that meet there, such as "Turn the cube slowly to show the corner where Yellow, Green and Red meet". See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A cube held on a corner is read three sides at a time.** When the cube is turned so that two or three sides show at once, each side that holds still now counts towards the scan, so holding one corner and then the corner across from it reads all six. Showing one side square to the camera at a time works exactly as before. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **German, French and Spanish.** Choose Deutsch, Français or Español in Settings and the app reads in that language, with the plural forms each has and numbers and dates in its own format; before this they changed only the layout direction and the language button, and every string stayed English. All three are first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through (as Japanese and Simplified Chinese are): expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. The app does not say so itself yet. See [docs/i18n.md](docs/i18n.md) for the words a reader should check first.
- **Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish are translated.** Choosing עברית, العربية or יידיש in Settings now shows the whole app in that language, laid out right to left, with the plural forms each language has (six for Arabic) and the cube's notation and your version and error codes kept left to right inside the text. They are first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through: expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. See [docs/i18n.md](docs/i18n.md).
- **A diagnostics channel for developers.** While the app runs, Settings > Developer (or Ctrl+Shift+D) can start writing what the scan reads and decides - each side's colours, how they were named, how the cube was put together, what the camera accepted, where the solve stands - to a file at a fixed place for the user, one JSON object per line, for another program or a person to read while the app is still up. A snapshot writes the whole current scan and solve as one record. It is off at every start unless `CUBE_DIAGNOSTICS` says otherwise, writes numbers and names and never a camera frame unless a separate switch is on, and replaces the `CUBE_HELD_LOG` file. See [docs/diagnostics.md](docs/diagnostics.md).
- **Review the solution opens the solution.** The button on the Solved screen was there but did nothing; it now opens every turn of the solve, grouped by stage with what each stage does and why, beside a cube that shows the position before the turn you have selected. Step with Previous and Next, press Play to watch the turns one after another, or tap a row to jump to it; the arrow keys, Space, Home and End do the same, and Back returns to the Solved screen. It keeps a place of its own, so looking back over a solve never moves the one you are doing.
- **A list of the cubes you have solved.** The History button on the start screen opens the solves kept on this device, newest first, each with its date, how many turns it took and how long. A solve left part-way is marked unfinished and carries on from the turn it reached; a finished one opens its solution to review. Each one can be deleted, and Settings has a switch to stop keeping a history and a button to clear it, both asking before anything is deleted. Only the colours of the cube, the turns and the time are kept, never a picture, and nothing leaves the device (`docs/design/screens/history.md`).
- **A user guide, with pictures.** Installing on each system, scanning a cube, following the solution, settings and keyboard shortcuts, what is kept and what is not, and what to do when a side will not read, each with pictures of the real screens. It is published with the documentation. See [docs/guide/index.md](docs/guide/index.md).
- **The browser version holds the camera's exposure and white balance for a scan, where the camera allows it.** A scan reads the same sticker on every side, and a camera's own exposure and white balance re-meter whenever a different colour fills the frame, so a colour could change between one side and the next. In Chrome and Edge the app now asks a camera that offers it to hold both where they settled, checks that it did, and gives them back when the scan ends; a browser or camera that does not offer it (Firefox and Safari do not) keeps its own automatics as before, and the scan goes on either way. What the camera allowed is in the [diagnostics](docs/diagnostics.md) record, and [docs/web.md](docs/web.md) says what differs from the desktop apps.

### Changed

- **The 3D cube no longer stalls the app on computers without a graphics card.** The sky that lights the cube is now a file made ahead of time, where Qt worked it out afresh each time a screen with the cube came up. With a graphics card that was not noticed; on a virtual machine, a remote desktop or in a browser without graphics acceleration it was seconds of every core each time the launch, solve or settings screen appeared, and in the browser half a minute of a page standing still at the start and the same again when it was closed. The cube looks the same.

### Fixed

- **The solved screen's figures can be read in the dark theme.** The box that shows your turns, time and method was drawn as a solid white rectangle under the dark theme's near-white text, so the figures were almost invisible; it now takes the page's own colour in both themes. The checkboxes in Settings had the same white fill and are fixed too.
- **A cube that six sides put together more than one way is settled by corner views held by hand.** The check that a corner view matches a candidate cube was tuned on frames of sides seen almost square-on and rejected every read of a cube held on a corner by hand under a lamp; it now allows for the colour drift of a side seen at an angle. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A logo on a centre sticker no longer tints it.** The white centre of a Rubik's cube carries a small multicoloured logo, and the scan read that tile darker and pinker than the whites around it, most of all by lamplight. Each sticker is now read from a wider patch and takes the colour most of it shares, so the logo is left out and the tile shows as the white it is. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Sides with near-black stickers are read more reliably.** Under a warm lamp a real cube's green and blue read almost black, and beside a white or yellow sticker the reader clipped them, so a side with a column or a row of them could be unreadable at the pose it was held in while the same side a quarter-turn away read. A frame the first look cannot make a side of is now looked at again with a narrower threshold, and what it finds is kept only if it sits on dark plastic. Over the recorded sessions 137 more frames read (2.5%), none that read before changed, and the green and blue sides of the warm-lamp session were read up to 2.5 times as often. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Two sides that look alike are no longer read as one.** A partly solved cube can have two sides that differ only in which stickers are red and which are orange. Under a warm lamp those two colours are close, and the scan took both sides for one, so it kept swapping red and orange as you turned the cube and never reached six sides. A side is now told by its centre sticker, and a side turned a quarter-turn is laid down by the stickers that tell the turns apart, so the scan completes. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A camera that would not start can be tried again from the scan screen.** When the camera was refused, missing or in use by another program, the scan screen said so and offered only Cancel, so you had to go back to the start and press Scan again. It now has a Try again button in the place Start over has, which asks for the camera again on the same screen: once permission is granted, or the other program lets go, the scan carries on.
- **A scan whose six sides do not make a cube no longer works the same answer out again for every frame.** When the sides that were read do not fit together - a misread side, or colours that drifted - the scan put them together afresh each time the camera moved a colour by a hair, which took up to about a tenth of a second per frame on a computer. It now does so again only when what was read has changed, and gives the same answer at the same frame as before. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A cube turned slowly about one axis and then another settles the scan.** The scan took two sides seen at much the same angle, the middle of every quarter-turn of a tumble, for one grid and read no corner at all, so a cube that its six sides put together more than one way stayed "more than one way" however it was turned. It now reads the two sides, keeps one read of each pair of sides seen (not the last few dozen reads of one pair), weighs every cube the six sides make (not the first sixteen), decides only when the views that fit leave one cube clear of the rest, and allows for a camera that runs at 20 frames a second or less, where a side turned at the same speed moves further between frames. A side seen at an angle in less light is no longer a seventh side. See [the investigation](docs/investigations/2026-10-14-scan-tumble.md) and [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **The solve screen's cube is clear of the camera preview and the move label.** On a phone, or a window dragged narrow, the camera's words ran across the cube's edge and the top of the cube sat under the move label; at 720 wide the words were on top of the stage label. The camera's words now sit under the move label, and the cube is given what the preview and the label leave it, so nothing is on anything. The cube is somewhat smaller on a short window and on the desktop layout, where it now sits below the preview rather than under it, and larger on a tall phone, which has the room.
- **A side turned to the edge of what the scan reads square-on is read.** In a cube turned slowly between two sides, one side spends part of every quarter-turn at about thirty degrees while the other is too thin to see. The reader laid a square grid on the stickers first, which is wrong for a side that is narrower one way or sheared, so a side inside the documented limit (a column step 0.85 of a row's, a cosine of 0.17) was read only up to about 0.87. When no side is held square it now places the stickers again, through the lattice they make, and refuses a fit that overlaps a better one that is not a side; a side that was read is read as before. On a drawn tumble 36 of 40 frames in that band read, up from 21; on the five recorded sessions it gains 11 reads and loses none, and the cube is known at the same frame. See [the investigation](docs/investigations/2026-10-15-square-on-dead-band.md) and [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A setting you change in the browser is kept even if you reload straight away.** Choosing a theme, a solving method or a language and then reloading or closing the tab within about half a second used to bring back the old choice, because the page had not yet written it. It is written as the page is hidden or left now. The app also reads its settings through Qt's current `Settings` type; what an earlier visit stored is read as before.
- **Colours are named by the cube's own white, so a warm lamp no longer confuses them.** Under a warm-white LED or a tungsten lamp every colour is pulled towards orange: the white reads pink, yellow reads as orange, and red and orange sit a few degrees apart. The scan now finds the white centre once all six sides are in, works out what the light did to it, and names every colour with that taken out; red and orange, which can no longer be told apart by an absolute hue, are told apart by which is yellower. Scans in daylight are unchanged. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **The scan no longer stalls the browser page as it starts, or grows slower the longer a cube is held.** In a browser the scan runs on the page's one thread, and the first frame of a scan took a quarter of a second of it (more than a second on a phone-class processor) building a colour table; a cube held in view cost the scan a little more every second; and putting six sides together that make no cube searched every turn of them one by one. The table is built while the camera opens, a held cube's colours are kept as running medians, and the search gives up a combination as soon as an edge or a corner of it is impossible, to the same answers. See [the measurement](docs/investigations/2026-10-15-web-scan-frame-cost.md).

### Security

- **Nothing is uploaded.** There is no account, no telemetry, no crash upload and no update check. The camera is read on the device and no picture from it is saved. A scan's frames reach the disk only when a developer sets the `CUBE_SCAN_RECORD_DIR` environment variable before the app starts, or switches on the diagnostics channel's separate frames option; the scan screen says so while either is on, and both are off at every start. See [docs/guide/privacy.md](docs/guide/privacy.md).
- **Every release file can be verified.** Each release carries a `SHA512SUMS` manifest, a GPG signature on it and on every package, an SBOM, and a build-provenance attestation that names the repository, commit and workflow that built each file. See [docs/SECURITY.md](docs/SECURITY.md#verifying-build-authenticity).

> **Pre-release.** The scan has been checked on recorded sessions of one cube in tungsten light and in backlit daylight, and on a webcam by hand; it has not been tried across webcams, rooms and cubes, and a cube with light-coloured plastic and flush tiles is not read. A sticker cannot be corrected by hand yet: one read wrongly means starting the scan again. The packages are not code-signed by Windows or Apple, so SmartScreen and Gatekeeper will ask before they run. The Linux and macOS builds are made and checked by CI, not yet run on hardware by the author.
