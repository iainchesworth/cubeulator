# Can the direct scan read a cube of N x N?

**Asked:** 2026-10-15 (gate S0 of [the N x N app plan](../nxn-app-plan.md)).
**Closed:** 2026-10-17, for what can be measured without a cube in the hand. **By:** an
agent working for the maintainer.

## What was tried

The plan asks, before any scan screen is built for another size: does the
reader find the stickers of a 5x5 and a 7x7, name them, and put six sides together
into the cube they came from? And since nothing in the repository was recorded of
a cube other than a 3x3, it can only be asked of drawings.

So the reader was made N-generic (`read_side(frame, size)`, `SideScan(size)`; the
change is described in [the vision pipeline](../vision-pipeline.md#a-cube-of-another-size)),
and `cube_scan_s0` (`tools/scan/s0.cpp`) was written to sweep it. For each size, light
and sticker spacing it scrambles cubes, draws their six sides one after another the
way a person shows them - each in a place of its own, turned up to 20 degrees in its
plane, squashed up to 8%, in a random quarter-turn, held for 12 frames that shake by a
pixel, with sensor noise - and runs the reader and the scan over the frames. The
lights are the test drawings' even daylight paints, and the six colours of a real cube
under a warm LED lamp as measured on 2026-10-14 (red and orange 16 Lab apart, green and
blue nearly black). Frames are 640 x 360, a 3x3's working size, unless said.

What each column means, and what is measured, are in the tool's header. In short:
`read` is the share of frames of a held side that found the side; `stkr` the share of
the stickers of those reads whose colour is the nearest of the six paints to the one
drawn (the reader's geometry and sampling, before any naming); `named` the share of
the stickers of a scan of six sides that the scan named right; and then how the scans
came out: the true cube, an ambiguous one, **a cube that is not the one drawn (a false
accept)**, none, or fewer than six sides captured.

The 3x3 is the control. It was replayed on every recorded 3x3 session available
(`cube_scan board` on the five sessions with a known cube, `replay` on the red/orange,
corner and tumble recordings and the two small corpora) before and after the change, and
every line of output is identical.

## What it found

**The reader reads a 5x5 and a 7x7 as it reads a 3x3, at the same sticker size.** Where a
cube is read at all, every sticker was the right colour: 100.00% over the sweep, 99.91%
at the very edge of what a 7x7 can be read at. Naming was right for every sticker of every
scan that captured six sides. Of 4,240 scans in the sweeps below that captured six sides,
all 4,240 put the true cube together; and in a further 3,600 (600 new cubes of each size in each light,
at half the frame's height, with twice the sensor noise) 3,588 did and 12 3x3s were
ambiguous, the one cube in eighty that six flat sides do not settle. **No scan put together a
wrong cube.** That is the plan's 99% bar, on drawings.

What limits it is how big a sticker is in the frame, not how many there are:

| | smallest sticker read (all 40 cubes) | cube's height as a share of the frame's |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 3x3, daylight | 14 px (pitch 18) | 15% |
| 3x3, warm lamp | 27 px (pitch 34) | 28% |
| 5x5, daylight | 13 px (pitch 16) | 22% |
| 5x5, warm lamp | 22 px (pitch 28) | 39% |
| 7x7, daylight | 13 px (pitch 16) | 31% |
| 7x7, warm lamp | 22 px (pitch 28) | 54% |

(Pitch is the distance between sticker centres, in the 640-wide working frame. A recorded
3x3's stickers are about 22 px across, a pitch of 27 and a quarter of the frame's height,
which is the scale to read this against. The warm lamp's dark greens and blues are what
costs: the reader needs the plastic between stickers, and in that light a dark sticker beside
a white one is clipped.) Stickers are 3/N of a 3x3's at the same extent, so **a 5x5 held as
a 3x3 is held is just readable in daylight and needs to be 1.7 times larger by a lamp, and a
7x7 needs 1.4 times in daylight and 2.4 times by a lamp**. Three things move the line, and
two are in:

- **The working width.** A 5x5 or 7x7 is worked at 960 or 1280 pixels wide (a 3x3 at 640,
  as always; a frame that is not that wide is read as it is). On 1280 x 720 frames a 7x7 is
  read in daylight from 25% of the frame and by a lamp from 30%, where at 640 it needs 31% and
  54%. It costs the pixels: one frame of a 5x5 is read in 3.7 ms at 960 wide (1.7 at 640) and
  of a 7x7 in 5.3 ms at 1280 (2.3 at 640), on the desktop, best of 60. **The platforms hand the
  reader 640 pixels today** (the browser's capture is capped there in `camera_web.js`), so this
  helps only where a camera gives more, until each platform is told the size.
- **The block** the frame is thresholded against is scaled with the sticker (3/N). Without it
  a 5x5 by a lamp read 87% of frames at pitch 28 (4 cubes of 24 captured) and needed 34; with it
  that is 100% (40 of 40). Daylight did not change.
- Not done: a smaller block still reads better on drawings (a 5x5 by lamp from pitch 16
  at half again), but those are drawings, and the 3x3's block was tuned on real light;
  scaling it with the sticker is the change that says "the same as a 3x3 of the same sticker
  size", which is the one that can be defended without recordings.

**The reader was wrong in one way the 3x3 could not be, and it is fixed.** The square
grid that the 3x3 is placed on first drops stickers from the outside in, because at four or
more cells from a seed a spacing a few per cent out has put a cell off its place. It dropped
whole rows, made up the span from strays, and returned a lattice one row from where the side
was, with 19 or 20 stickers where there were 25, frame after frame alternating with the right
one. The steadiness gate then saw the side jump a row, and two cubes in 24 never captured a
sixth side. A cube of more than three is now placed through its own fit
(`fit_lattice_through_fit`) from the start.

**What the scan can and cannot see wrong.** The assembly asks `cube::nxn::validate` of each of
its 4^6 turns of the sides; whether it accepts a cube that is not the one in the hand is a
property of the stickers, not the camera. Two stickers of different colours swapped (the
error the exactly-N^2-of-each-colour naming makes), over 100 cubes and 400 swaps each:

| size | swap of two | still a cube |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 3x3 | any two stickers | 0 of 33,262 |
| 4x4 | two wing stickers | 7.2% |
| 4x4 | two centre stickers | 100% |
| 5x5 | two wing stickers | 7.4% |
| 5x5 | two centre stickers | 49.7% |
| 7x7 | two wing stickers | 3.7% |
| 7x7 | two centre stickers | 16.4% |

The centre stickers of a ring have no identity of their own, so nothing about the cube can say
that two are traded: a 4x4's centres are all exchangeable, and so are those of a ring on the
bigger cubes. A 3x3 scan is held to its pieces and its fixed centres; a bigger cube cannot be.
**Whether a bigger cube's scan is the cube in the hand rests on the stickers having been named
right, and a review screen is the check.** No misnaming was seen in the sweep, so how often it
would be on a real cube is not known.

**The steadiness gate gets harder with the size.** A read counts only if the side moved less
than 0.12 of a sticker spacing since the frame before, which is a smaller number of pixels for a
smaller sticker. With shake that is independent from frame to frame, at 50% of the frame, as the share of the frames of a hold that were counted (the
first frame of a hold never can be: the most is 92% of 12 frames, 96% of 24):

| shake (uniform, +-px) | 3x3 | 5x5 | 7x7 |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.5 | 92% | 92% | 92% |
| 2 | 96% | 93% | 63% |
| 4 | 82% | 29% | 11% |

At +-4 px a 7x7 counts about one frame in nine and so takes about 70 frames (two to three
seconds) to capture a side, where a 3x3 takes ten. What a real hand does is not known, and the
number is one constant (`kSteadyFraction`), which the 3x3 shares.

**Even sizes work too, and a 2x2 is ambiguous one time in five.** A 2x2, 4x4 and 6x6 have no
middle sticker: nothing seeds a colour's cluster, says which side of the cube a read is, or pins a side
to a place. Two changes make up for it. The colours are found from the 24 corner stickers, which are the
same four cells of a side however it is turned and four of each colour, clustered in groups of four from
the best of 24 farthest-first starts, and those six means seed the rest. And the sides are put on the cube
by trying: the first on the top, the other five on any free face in four turns (122,880 at the most), a
branch stopping where a corner or wing shows a colour twice, `validate` reading the colour scheme off the
corners at the end. The cost is not in the search (145 cubes of 2x2, 4x4 and 6x6 in 0.6 s). Drawn,
in the same sweeps as the odd sizes:

| | read | named | exact | ambiguous | wrong |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 4x4, 600 cubes in each light, half the frame | 100% | 100% | 1,200 | 0 | 0 |
| 6x6, the same | 100% | 100% | 1,200 | 0 | 0 |
| 2x2, the same | 100% | 100% | 957 | 235 | 0 |

The 2x2's six flat sides make more than one cube (up to eight) about one time in five, which is
the cube's: its sides lose which way up each was held, and it has no centres, wings or edges left to say.
No corner view exists above a 3x3, so the review screen is what settles it. A 2x2's smallest readable sticker is
14 px in daylight (pitch 18, 10% of the frame's height) and 32 px by a lamp; a 4x4's is 13 px (pitch 16) and
22 px (pitch 28); a 6x6's 13 px and 22 px, which is the same sticker size as the odd sizes. Swapping any two
stickers of a 2x2 is seen (0 of 33,250 pass `validate`); at a 6x6 centre swaps are not (25%), wing swaps are
4%. A 4x4 and 6x6 at +-4 px of shake count 46% and 17% of frames.

## Decision

- **S0 passes offline for odd sizes** (3, 5, 7), on drawings, against the plan's 99% sticker
  bar, with no wrong cube in 4,240 scans. It does **not** pass the plan's third item - real
  recordings of a 5x5 and a 7x7 - because none exist. **Nothing here says a 5x5 can be read in a
  real room.** Light, glare, stickerless plastic, thin gaps between stickers, a hand, and how
  closely a person will hold a 5x5 are all unmeasured.
- A scan screen for 5x5 and 7x7 is therefore worth building but not worth shipping as read-and-done:
  it should open the colours review (the one place a swap of centres can be seen), and tell the
  person to hold a 5x5 (a 7x7) close, about half the frame by a lamp.
- The platforms' capture size is the next thing to change (640 wide today), for a 7x7 first.
- Even sizes (P3) are done offline too, by the corners: a 4x4 and a 6x6 read and assemble as the odd
  sizes do. A 2x2 ends ambiguous one time in five, and the answer to that is the review screen, which
  a scan of any size reaches.

## Where the code is

`tools/scan/s0.cpp` (`cube_scan_s0`): `--sizes 3,5,7 --extents 25,30,40,50,60,75 --cubes 60
--light both` is the main table; `--jitter`, `--noise`, `--blur`, `--frame 1280x720` and
`--oracle 3,4,5,7` give the others, and `--bench 5 1280x720 34` what a frame costs.
`tests/unit/direct_scan_nxn_test.cpp` holds the size-generic behaviour in place;
`tests/unit/nxn/scan_assembly_test.cpp` the assembly.
